Speed

Identify a moment when chance, agency, or risk changed your players experience in either speed or the game you deconstructed in class. How did the system support—or disrupt—flow? Use Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs or another course concept to explain one source of player motivation, then propose one adjustment that could make the experience feel fairer or more engaging.

Design

For me, I felt as though there wasn't enough strategic agency when I realized that, despite being able to place down cards to "block" my opponent, there wasn't really much rhyme or reason for how I could do so: I have little knowledge of what cards my opponent actually has, so it's mainly a question of just playing my own cards as fast as possible. While that does have its own appeal, I thought it would be good to resonate more strongly with the Esteem level of Maslow's Hierarchy, as I feel that strength over others should also stem from the analyzation of others. Otherwise, I might as well play Speed against a CPU, no? In any case, I feel that "flow" starts to be lost once you finish playing all of the cards in your hand, and you have to sort of dawdle while waiting for your opponent to play their cards.

To bring about my desired, tactical nature to Speed, I think a possible adjustment would be to allow players to set down sequences of 3+ cards face-up in order to draw more cards sooner. This sequence could be placed (not on top of the piles) even if the piles would not normally allow for their acceptance, but because it is face-up, the opposing player can try to develop tactics to prevent it from being accepted. If a sequence can be activated, it should take priority over any normal card placements (and sequences cannot interject into other sequences)

Example scenario:
Piles: 1c and 10d (c = clubs, h = hearts, etc)
P1 has a hand composed of 3c4s5c7h7s
P2 has a hand composed of 2h3h5d7d9d

P1 might set down 3c4s5c to draw 3 more cards and accelerate their hand.
P2, seeing this new sequence, can now be much more reluctant to place down 2h onto 1c even if it could let them place 3h as it would activate the sequence first. They can instead choose to place down 9d onto 10d and hope that their next draw can help counter it (ideally a 4, which would let them place a 2345 sequence).
Without this kind of system and extra information for P2, then they are very simply inclined to place down 2h into 3h, to which P1 will just quickly place down 3 4 and 5.
Is this inspired by Mahjong? Not directly by any particular mechanic, but I think there may be some similarities in terms of core design around tile calling...
