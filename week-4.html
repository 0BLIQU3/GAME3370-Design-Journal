UEFN Escape Room

How did the UEFN Escape room interface, feedback, collection, or progression systems teach you what to do and influence what you wanted to do next? Analyze one specific gameplay moment, then identify one accessibility or onboarding change that could improve the experience for a particular player.

Design

When it came to playing through the UEFN Escape Room, there was certainly a clear enough line of progression/feedback to help lead the player along: by simply just walking around the building, you could run into locked doors that would display an indicator of the necessary item/key to open them, and the necessary items were generally well highlighted relative to their surrounding.

However, there was one specific gameplay moment that I found to be an issue, that being the welding torch required to open the final safe. This welding torch was positioned off to the side of the safe room, opposite of the prevailing puzzle elements and lacked a highlight for its location. Additionally, the safe did not display that a welding torch was actually necessary for it, and given that a welding torch is not exactly the most intuitive solution to opening a safe (as opposed to some manner of key/code), it was tricky to realize that it was the proper solution.

Additionally, I was also getting confused by very quick action prompts for the vault movement action while I was exploring the safe room (I only realized that's what these prompts were after the escape room, as the action prompt flashes on-screen so quickly that you cannot actually interpret what it is for). This caused me to believe that, in order to open the safe, perhaps I needed to interact with something within the safe's close vicinity (like a secret lever of sorts) rather than something across the room entirely. Upon consultation with my classmates, it also seemed that I was not the only one that struggled with this issue, and I even had the opportunity of observing a classmate get stuck on just this one part of the escape room in real time.

Apart from adding additional highlight elements and more clarity in what's necessary to open the safe, it's worth noting that vaulting was not even an action necessary to solve the escape room, so it now runs a greater risk of being a conflicting element. Simply put, I find that this experience shows how important it is to cull any unnecessary mechanics that could potentially distract a player.
