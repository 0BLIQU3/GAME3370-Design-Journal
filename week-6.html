Juice

Design

To "juice" a game is to introduce additional sensory feedback elements to assist with convey game mechanics. It's not something entirely necessary for the game mechanics to function, but it is something that can significantly impact the overall game experience and how those mechanics are understood. For my own interpretation of juice, however, I think it is best when it is used to help display "invisible" or "magic" numbers: rather than constantly throwing numbers onto screen to convey the severity of certain effects (ex: damage of an attack, current speed of the player), I think propre "juice" is when VFX/SFX is utilized to portray this instead.
Simple examples I would consider using: crunchy sounds and sharp/flashy effects to scale with damage, air particles and wind blowing sounds to convey high speeds, or dampening/filtering other sounds to convey low speed.

I would definitely like to stress that sound is equally important for creating "juice" compared to visual FX. It engages a sense just as important as your sight: your hearing. I believe that, while sight is perhaps the #1 sense used for video games, sound is powerful enough to independently paint a picture of a scene in a listener's head. Sound can help convey an action, the subject/victim of an action, perhaps even the setting an action takes place in, so I think pairing a simple graphic + a simple sound can be more impactful than a more complex graphic alone.

Now, sound is one of the most important parts of juice in my opinion, but one thing that stood out to me within that video was screenshake in particular, because this seemed to actually have rather negative effects upon the game experience. This is not to say that juice or screenshake are entirely inappropriate: I think it would've been reasonable if the screenshake only occurred after a prolonged "combo" of sorts, to help play off of tension for a big score, but it is a good example of how juice can have drawbacks if misused.
